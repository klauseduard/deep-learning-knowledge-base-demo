> Using models to generate training data for other models — from GPT-4-distilled instruction datasets (Alpaca, Orca) to "textbook-quality" pre-training corpora (Phi), self-play for reasoning, and the model collapse risk from recursive synthetic training.

## Connections

- [[knowledge-distillation]] — synthetic data generation is distillation at scale: the teacher model's knowledge is captured in generated data rather than soft labels. The Phi models show this can be more effective than traditional distillation
- [[data-engineering]] — synthetic data is the newest and fastest-growing source in the data engineering pipeline; it complements web scraping, human annotation, and programmatic labeling
- [[ai-safety-and-alignment]] — Constitutional AI and RLAIF use synthetic data for alignment; the quality of synthetic preference data determines alignment quality
- [[reasoning-in-llms]] — reasoning models use self-generated traces as training data (STaR, rejection sampling); the boundary between synthetic data and RL exploration is blurring
- [[scaling-laws]] — Phi models suggest that data quality can substitute for model scale, potentially changing the compute-optimal frontier; model collapse suggests limits to synthetic data scaling
- [[large-language-models]] — most open-source LLMs (Alpaca, Vicuna, Orca, WizardLM) derive their instruction-following ability from synthetic data generated by frontier models
- [[training-techniques]] — synthetic data is used in SFT, DPO preference generation, and RLHF reward model training; it's woven into every stage of the post-training pipeline
- [[diffusion-models]] — synthetic images from diffusion models can train vision models; StableRep demonstrates that synthetic-only visual representation learning is competitive
